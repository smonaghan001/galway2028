While the mountain runners were conquering Romania, another group of Irish masters athletes were delivering outstanding performances at the BMAF Track & Field Championships in Derby this past weekend — bringing home **15 medals** across a wide range of events.

## The Standout Performances

**Anne Gilshinan** (W60) was the star of the Irish contingent, winning **Gold** in the 800m (2:27.85) and **Gold** in the 1500m (5:02.01) — a magnificent double on the track.

**Tom O'Brien** (M55) was equally impressive in the field, taking **Gold** in the High Jump (1.55m) and **Silver** in the Long Jump (5.24m).

**Damian Crawford** (M55) won **Gold** in the Javelin, adding to what was a superb weekend for the M55 age group.

**Ger Cremin** (M45) won **Gold** in the 110m Hurdles — finishing 3rd overall in the combined M35-45 race but claiming the M45 age group title with a time of 17.09.

**Ciara Burrows** (W40) had a brilliant weekend in the throws, winning **Bronze in both the Discus and the Shot Put** in the W40 age group.

**Noelle O'Regan** (W35) took a fine **Silver** in the 400m, running 1:01.20, while **Lynsey Glover** (W35) won **Silver** in the Weight Throw.

**Keith Pollard** (M45) earned two bronze medals — in the 200m (23.88) and the 400m (54.15).

**Robert Bruce-Brand** (M55) claimed **Bronze** in the 400m M55 (58.50), **Eoghan Murphy** (M35) took **Bronze** in the 800m (2:22.45), and **Alison Beck** (W45) rounded off the haul with **Bronze** in the High Jump (1.40m).

## An Irish Masters Weekend to Remember

Taken together with the EMORRC results from Romania, this was one of the finest weekends Irish masters athletics has produced in a long time — medals across mountain running and track & field, with athletes competing from M35 through to W60 age groups.

The depth and quality on show is a real statement of intent for Irish masters athletics, and a tremendous encouragement as we build towards the European Masters Athletics Championships Non-Stadia in Galway in May 2028.

We hope to see many of these athletes — and many more — on the start line in Galway.
