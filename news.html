23 August 2026

Medals for Ireland in Daegu — Live Updates

The World Masters Athletics Championships in Daegu run until 3 September, and Ireland's team of 28 has won fourteen medals — six gold, four silver and four bronze. That is enough to place Ireland 23rd of the 106 countries competing, from one of the smaller teams here. We'll keep updating this page as results come in, so check back, and follow us on Facebook and Instagram for news from the ground.

Irish medals so far
🥇 John MacDermott — Gold, M80 Decathlon (4878 pts)
🥇 John MacDermott — Gold, M80 200m Hurdles (37.71)
🥇 Sean McMullin — Gold, M80 10km Road Walk
🥇 Sean McMullin — Gold, M80 20km Road Walk (2:41:51)
🥇 Edel Maguire — Gold, W65 High Jump (1.36)
🥇 Rachel Murphy — Gold, W40 1500m (5:02.60)
🥈 Pat Kelly — Silver, W75 100m (18.03)
🥈 Edel Maguire — Silver, W65 200m (29.86)
🥈 Lea Fulcher — Silver, M50 1500m (4:15.85)
🥈 Brenda Gannon — Silver, W50 20km Road Walk (2:15:54)
🥉 Stephen Hunter — Bronze, M45 3000m Steeplechase (11:27.98)
🥉 Edel Maguire — Bronze, W65 100m (14.51)
🥉 Rachel Murphy — Bronze, W40 5000m (19:38.78)
🥉 Shane Sheridan, Niall Kelly, Richard Phelan and Stephen Berkeley — Bronze, M65 4x100m Relay

Two world titles on the road, at eighty. Sean McMullin won the M80 10km walk early in the championships and then came back for the 20km, covering it in 2:41:51 to take a second gold. Twenty kilometres of race walking is a punishing way to spend a morning at any age; every stride of it is judged, and the technique has to hold when the legs have stopped cooperating. Brenda Gannon took silver in the W50 20km in 2:15:54 on the same course.

And Edel Maguire has won a full set. Bronze over 100m, silver over 200m, then gold in the W65 high jump at 1.36m — three medals, three colours, on the track and in the field, with the 400m still to come. Rachel Murphy has two of her own: gold in the W40 1500m and bronze in the 5000m. Lea Fulcher took silver in a quick M50 1500m, running 4:15.85.

John MacDermott has two golds. The M80 decathlon — ten events across two days, at eighty-one, for 4878 points — and then the 200m hurdles in 37.71. He won five of the ten decathlon disciplines outright along the way, and he still has the 400m to come. It is a reminder, if any were needed, that masters athletics is not a gentle wind-down but a serious competitive sport, contested by people who have kept at it for decades.

Ireland's first medal of the championships came on the opening morning and deserves more notice than it got at the time: Stephen Hunter took bronze in the M45 3000m steeplechase in 11:27.98 on 22 August. Seven and a half laps, twenty-eight barriers and seven water jumps — the steeplechase asks you to run hard and then, twenty-eight times, to stop thinking about how tired you are and clear an obstacle that will not move.

The sprints have been good to Ireland too. Pat Kelly took silver in the W75 100m in 18.03 against the best in the world in her age group. Add a strong Irish showing in the 10km road race in tough, humid conditions, and it has been a championships to be proud of.

And then the relay. Shane Sheridan, Niall Kelly, Richard Phelan and Stephen Berkeley took bronze in the M65 4x100m — four men, three baton changes, and a world championship medal that belongs to all of them equally. There is something particular about a relay medal: the individual events reward a lifetime of your own work, but the relay only happens if four people get it right together, on the same evening, in the same forty-odd seconds. The photograph of the four of them afterwards tells you everything about what it meant.

Members of the Galway 2028 organising committee are in Daegu — watching, competing, and learning from a championship being hosted superbly. Many of the athletes racing in Korea will be with us in Galway in May 2028 for the European Masters Non-Stadia Championships. Follow the journey at galway2028.ie.

19 August 2026

Ireland Goes to Daegu — and We're Going With Them

This week, the masters athletics world gathers in Daegu, South Korea. The World Masters Athletics Championships open with a ceremony on 21 August and run through to 3 September — the largest edition ever staged in Asia, with 7,476 athletes entered from 106 countries. Among them, twenty-eight Irish athletes have entered — carrying the green vest a very long way from home.

It is worth pausing on what that actually means. These are people in their thirties, forties, fifties, sixties, seventies and beyond, who have organised their training, their work, their families and their savings around a start line on the other side of the world. Some will come home with medals. Every one of them has already done the hard part. To all of them, and to everyone else lining up in Daegu — from all of us involved with Galway 2028, the very best of luck.

Galway 2028 will be there too. Members of our organising committee are travelling to Daegu to watch, to learn, compete, and to meet the athletes and officials who make this sport what it is. We'll be sharing photos and stories from the ground throughout the championships, so follow along on Facebook and Instagram if you'd like to see Daegu through Irish eyes.

Why does a non-stadia championship in Ireland care so much about a stadia championship in Korea? Because it's the same community. The athletes racing in Daegu are the athletes who'll be racing the roads, the cross country and the walking courses of Galway in May 2028 — and there is no better place to learn how to host them well than at a championship being hosted brilliantly. Follow the journey at galway2028.ie.

11 August 2026

Discover Galway: A Host City Worth the Journey

When you line up in Galway in May 2028, you'll be racing in one of Europe's most characterful corners. Galway is a compact, walkable city on Ireland's west coast — cobbled streets, live music spilling from every doorway, and the salt-air freshness of the Atlantic just beyond. For masters athletes travelling from across Europe, it's a destination that rewards you long after the finish line.

The setting is part of the appeal. Galway sits at the heart of the Wild Atlantic Way, with Connemara, the Burren and the Aran Islands all within easy reach. The same coastline and countryside that make the region so memorable to visit are exactly what make it a superb stage for non-stadia athletics — road running, cross country and race walking through scenery you'll want to photograph as much as race through.

Getting here is easier than you might think. Galway is well connected by road and rail to Dublin and Shannon airports, with Ireland West Airport at Knock offering direct European links closer to the city. Many athletes make a week of it — a few days competing, a few days exploring — and the warm West of Ireland welcome makes it easy to bring family and supporters along.

We'll be sharing more travel, accommodation and things-to-do guidance as 2028 approaches. For now, start dreaming — this is a city that knows how to make visitors feel at home. Keep up to date at galway2028.ie.

30 July 2026

Rooted in Tradition — The Story Behind Non-Stadia Championships

When masters athletes line up in Galway in May 2028, they'll be taking part in something with real history behind it. The European Non-Stadia Championships were first held in 1989, and have run as a biennial fixture ever since — one of the longest-standing traditions in European masters athletics.

"Non-stadia" simply means events held away from the track: road racing over 10K and half marathon distances, cross country relay, and race walking. It's a different test to stadium athletics — pace judgement, terrain, weather, and tactics all come into play — and it draws a slightly different, often larger, crowd of masters athletes who love the open road as much as the oval track. One of the most popular recent additions to the programme is Nordic walking, which has quickly become a firm fixture on the non-stadia calendar — and Galway 2028 will be no exception.

Over nearly four decades, the championships have travelled across Europe, building a loyal community that returns edition after edition. Galway will host the 21st edition in 2028 — and the first time this championship has come to Ireland.

14 July 2026

Irish Masters Are on a Roll

It's been a summer to remember for Irish masters athletics — and every result is another reason to be excited about what's coming to Galway in May 2028.

At the World Masters Mountain Running Championships in Janské Lázně, Czech Republic (26–28 June), Irish teams struck gold twice: John Kinsella, Eoin Flynn and Ian Conroy took the M45 uphill title, while Carmel MacDomhnaill was part of the winning W80 uphill team. Northern Ireland's Springwell Running Club also had a brilliant championship, with Carolyn Crawford, Kate O'Loan, Sarah Milligan and Adele Tomb bringing home four medals between them, competing in searing 36°C heat on the slopes of Černá hora.

Earlier in the summer, Mayo AC's Paraic Sweeney won gold in the M75 10km road race at EMACNS Catania 2026 — the very championship Galway will host next, in its 21st edition.

Different disciplines, different countries, same message: Irish masters athletics has real depth across the age categories. We can't wait to see Irish athletes competing on home soil when the championship arrives in Galway in under two years. Well done to everyone who represented Ireland this summer — if you have a story of your own, tag us, we'd love to help tell it.

30 June 2026

Galway City Council Confirms Full Backing for EMACNS 2028

We are delighted to share that Galway City Council has officially confirmed its full support for the European Masters Athletics Championships Non-Stadia — and to mark the occasion, a formal launch took place in Galway with Cllr. Mike Cubbard, Mayor of Galway, and Sally Ann O'Brien, Head of Tourism, Galway City Council. Pictured above are committee members alongside the Mayor and the official EMA flag that will fly over Galway in May 2028.

The successful bid was led by the Local Organising Committee in partnership with Galway City Council, Athletics Ireland, and the Irish Masters Athletics Association, and was confirmed by European Masters Athletics following its 2026 event in Catania, Italy. The championships — the 21st edition of this biannual event — are expected to attract approximately 1,000 athletes from across Europe, with total visitor numbers, including families and supporters, estimated at around 3,000.

Cllr. Mike Cubbard said: "This is a fantastic achievement for Galway and a proud moment for our city. The Championships will bring thousands of athletes and visitors to Galway, showcasing our community spirit, our coastal setting and our ability to deliver major international events. We look forward to welcoming Europe's masters' athletes in 2028." Sally Ann O'Brien, Head of Tourism, added: "This is a strategically important win for Galway. Participation events like this deliver real economic impact, extend the tourism season and highlight Galway as a world-class destination for sporting events."

Athletes can look forward to road racing, cross country relay, race walking, and Nordic walking over three days in May 2028. Detailed race routes, venues, and operational plans will be confirmed and published in the months ahead. Follow galway2028.ie and our social channels to stay up to date as announcements land.

15 June 2026

Galway Gears Up — A City Built for Champions

With exactly 23 months to go until May 2028, planning for the European Masters Athletics Championships Non-Stadia is gathering pace — and we want to tell you a little more about the city that will be your home for those unforgettable days of competition.

Galway is one of Europe's most celebrated cultural cities: a vibrant, walkable city on Ireland's Wild Atlantic Way, with a festival spirit that runs through its very streets. From the medieval Latin Quarter and the Claddagh waterfront to the world-class Galway Racecourse and the wide open spaces of Connacht Athletics Arena, the city has everything a masters athlete could ask for — outstanding venues, a warm welcome, and an atmosphere that simply has to be experienced.

The organising committee is deep in the planning process, working closely with Athletics Ireland, local authorities, and venue partners to ensure that EMACNS 2028 delivers a championship worthy of its athletes. In the months ahead we will be sharing confirmed venue details, the event programme, and all the practical information you need to begin planning your trip to Ireland.

Whether you are a seasoned masters competitor or attending your first European championship, we promise you an event to remember. Mark May 2028 in your diary, follow our updates, and join us as Galway prepares to welcome the best masters athletes in Europe.
